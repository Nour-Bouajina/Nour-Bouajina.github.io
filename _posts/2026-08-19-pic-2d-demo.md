`demo.ipynb`, in the [PIC repo](https://github.com/Nour-Bouajina/PIC) under `idea1/`, shrinks the real problem, `theta in R^42 -> S(theta) in R^400`, down to `theta in R^2` with a low-dimensional simulator output, small enough to plot directly. Nothing here is a simplification of the *math*, the KL objective, the prior, beta, the flow are all the real thing, just in 2 dimensions instead of 42, so every claim can be looked at on a plot instead of inferred from a loss curve. This post explains the logic behind each experiment and reports what actually happened when the notebook was run (not what should happen in principle).

## Why two simulators

**Demo A**, complex squaring, gives a *discrete* one-to-many inverse: exactly 2 solutions per target. It is the simplest possible setting to see collapse in.

**Demo B**, `theta_1^2 + theta_2^2`, gives a *continuous* solution manifold: a whole circle of solutions per target. This is the closer analogue to the real project. 42 knobs against 400 output constraints generically leaves a positive-dimensional solution set too (not isolated points), exactly like 2 knobs against 1 output constraint leaves a 1-dimensional circle (`2 - 1 = 1`). Demo B is the main experiment; the flow, the KL objective, and conditioning are all built and tested there.

## Demo A: discrete multiple solutions

`S_A(theta_1, theta_2) = (theta_1^2 - theta_2^2, 2*theta_1*theta_2)`, which is `(theta_1 + i*theta_2)^2` written out in real/imaginary form. In polar coordinates this simulator doubles the angle, so `S_A(theta) = S_A(-theta)` always: any nonzero target already has two solutions, 180 degrees apart.

**Ordinary inverse optimization.** For the target `y* = S_A(1,1) = (0,2)`, 24 random starting points were optimized by plain gradient descent on `d(theta) = ||S_A(theta) - y*||^2`. Result: **10 converged near `(1,1)`, 14 near `(-1,-1)`**, roughly split between the two attractor basins, exactly the two known exact solutions. Different starts, different (but both valid) answers.

**Naive generator, physics-only.** A tiny MLP `theta = f_phi(z)`, `z` 1-D, trained only against `L_physics = mean(d(S_A(theta_i), y*))`. After training: final physics loss **0.000046** (it found a valid solution), but **100% of generated samples landed near `(-1,-1)` and 0% near `(1,1)`**. Complete collapse onto a single one of the two solutions, even though nothing in the loss prefers one over the other, the optimizer just broke the symmetry arbitrarily and had zero incentive to represent both.

## Demo B: continuous solution manifold (the main experiment)

`S_B(theta) = theta_1^2 + theta_2^2`. For target `y* = 4`, the exact solution set is `theta_1^2 + theta_2^2 = 4`, a circle of radius 2. The physics loss `d(theta) = (S_B(theta) - y*)^2` plotted over the plane is a ring-shaped valley, not a single minimum, the picture that motivates everything downstream.

**Ordinary inverse optimization.** 30 random starts, gradient descent on `d(theta)`. Final angles landed all over the circle (spread across the full 0 to 360 degree range, no clustering), confirming "one target, many valid solutions" experimentally, not just by construction.

**Naive generator, physics-only.** Same recipe as Demo A, transplanted onto the circle. Final physics loss **0.001682**, but the generated points' angular spread was **std = 28.1 degrees**, far tighter than the roughly 104 degrees a uniform spread over a full circle would give. The generator solved the inverse problem (low physics error) without learning the inverse solution *distribution* (it found one point on the circle and stuck to it). The notebook's training-progress panel shows this happening live: early samples are scattered, later samples visibly converge onto one point.

## p(theta | y*): plotted directly

With only 2 dimensions, `p(theta | y*) ~ p(theta) * exp(-beta * d(S(theta), y*))` can be evaluated on a grid and shown as a heatmap, no sampling needed.

- **Uniform prior, beta = 10**: probability concentrates in a smooth band around the solution circle, not sharply on it (finite beta gives a "soft" solution set), and is flat in angle, matching the rotational symmetry of both the prior and `d`.
- **Gaussian prior vs. uniform prior**, same target and beta: the Gaussian prior visibly tilts the density toward the side of the ring closer to the origin, without changing which configurations are valid. This is the prior's actual job: not deciding validity, deciding how probability is distributed among valid configurations.
- **beta in {0.1, 1, 10, 100}**: at beta = 0.1 the density is almost as broad as the whole prior box; at beta = 100 it's a razor-thin ring. The transition from "physics barely matters" to "physics is nearly a hard constraint" is continuous and visible.

## The flow

`ConditionalFlow2D` in the notebook is a direct 2-D shrink of the real project's conditional normalizing flow: affine coupling layers conditioned on an embedding of `y*`, a tanh squash into `(-3, 3)^2` matching the uniform prior's support (same role as the real model's sigmoid squash into `[theta_min, theta_max]`), both directions implemented with log-determinants tracked through the change-of-variables formula.

**Self-consistency check**: `sample()`'s forward-pass `log_q` and `log_prob()`'s independent inverse-pass evaluation agreed to **2.4e-7** on a random batch. The two code paths compute the same density.

**Training against the KL objective**, fixed target `y* = 4`, uniform prior, beta = 10, 4000 steps: loss went from **223.2 at step 0 to -0.385 final**, mean physics error settled around **0.06 to 0.07**.

**Learned q_phi vs. true p, side by side**: a 20000-sample 2D histogram of the trained flow's output visually reproduces the ring shape of the analytic heatmap, concentrated around radius 2, spread across the full range of angles rather than piled at one point.

**Validity** (2000 fresh samples): mean `|S(theta_i) - y*|` = **0.190**. Not exact, expected at a finite beta that deliberately trades some accuracy for spread (matches the beta sweep's lesson directly).

**Diversity / coverage**: angular std = **90.4 degrees**, versus 28.1 degrees for the collapsed physics-only generator and roughly 104 degrees for a perfectly uniform spread. The KL-trained flow lands closer to full coverage than not, though not perfectly uniform, a real, honest result rather than a clean textbook number, worth noticing rather than rounding away.

## One model, multiple targets

A single `q_phi(theta | y)` was trained on `y* in {1, 2, 4, 9}` (radii 1, 1.414, 2, 3) at once, then tested on those four plus one **unseen** value, `y* = 6.25` (radius 2.5), never shown during training.

| y* | true radius | mean generated radius | mean \|S(theta)-y*\| |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.00 | 1.000 | 0.999 | 0.165 |
| 2.00 | 1.414 | 1.413 | 0.175 |
| 4.00 | 2.000 | 1.985 | 0.196 |
| 9.00 | 3.000 | 2.996 | 0.189 |
| **6.25 (unseen)** | **2.500** | **2.663** | **0.937** |

The four trained targets match their true radius almost exactly (largest deviation 0.015) with validity errors all in the same 0.16 to 0.20 range as the single-target experiment above, evidence the conditioning genuinely works, not just that the model memorized four separate rings, it's one shared set of weights producing the right radius for each input. The unseen target generalizes in the right direction (2.663 is much closer to 2.5 than to any trained radius) but with a validity error roughly 5x worse than the trained targets. That's a real, informative gap: the conditional mapping interpolates reasonably but not for free, exactly the kind of thing worth checking (and reporting honestly) when scaling this idea up to the real 42-to-400 problem with arbitrary, never-before-seen target spectra.

## What this maps back to in the real project

| Proposal concept | Demo evidence |
|---|---|
| one-to-many inverse | Demo A's 10/14 split across 2 solutions; Demo B's 30 distinct optimization endpoints on one circle |
| solution manifold dimension = knobs minus constraints | Demo B: 2 - 1 = 1, a literal 1-D circle |
| physics-only collapse | both naive generators: near-zero physics loss, near-total loss of diversity (100% and std=28 deg respectively) |
| p(theta \| y*) and the role of beta | grid heatmaps, broad at beta=0.1 to razor-thin at beta=100 |
| role of the prior | uniform vs. Gaussian prior visibly reshaping (not invalidating) the ring |
| conditional normalizing flow, dim z = dim theta | `ConditionalFlow2D`, verified self-consistent to 2.4e-7 |
| KL-derived training objective | loss 223 to -0.385; validity 0.19, diversity std=90 deg (vs. collapsed 28 deg) |
| one model, many targets | 4 trained targets matched to within 0.015 of true radius; 1 unseen target generalized with a real but visible validity gap |

Same failure modes, same objective, same architecture pattern as the real 42-to-400 project, just small enough to watch happen on a plot instead of inferring from a number.
